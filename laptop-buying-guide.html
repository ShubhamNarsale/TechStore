Laptop Buying Guide

A practical checklist for choosing a laptop based on what you actually plan to do with it.

Start with your workload

A good laptop choice begins with the tasks you need it to handle. A student writing assignments has different priorities from a programmer, creator or gamer. Compare your needs before focusing on individual specifications.

Key specifications to understand

Processor

The processor handles general computing work. Consider the type of applications you use and the level of multitasking you expect rather than choosing a processor only by its name.

RAM

RAM is the working memory used by active programs. More RAM can help when you keep multiple applications or browser tabs open. Read the RAM Guide for a closer look.

Storage

Storage holds your operating system, applications and files. SSD storage is commonly selected when fast everyday access is important. See SSD vs HDD for a direct comparison.

Display

Look at screen size, resolution, panel characteristics and refresh rate according to your work. A comfortable display can matter as much as raw performance for long study or office sessions.

Battery and portability

If you carry the laptop between classrooms, offices or other locations, weight, dimensions and battery expectations become important. Higher-performance hardware can also affect battery expectations.

Graphics

Integrated graphics can suit many everyday workloads, while a dedicated GPU can be important for gaming and some creative applications. Match graphics hardware to the software you actually use.

Operating requirements

Check the operating system and software requirements of your important applications before buying. Compatibility matters more than a generic specification list.

Budget considerations

Set a realistic budget and prioritize the components that directly affect your workload. Avoid paying for specifications you are unlikely to use.

Choose by use case

Students

Prioritize portability, battery expectations, comfortable display, adequate RAM and storage, and a budget that fits your course needs. See the Student Laptop Guide.

Office users

For documents, web applications, meetings and everyday multitasking, balanced performance, comfortable display, connectivity and portability can be more useful than specialized graphics hardware.

Programmers

Consider processor capability, RAM, SSD storage, display space and connectivity for development tools, local environments and multitasking.

Creators

Check application requirements carefully. CPU performance, RAM, fast storage, display characteristics and graphics capability may all matter depending on the creative workload.

Gamers

Gaming usually makes graphics capability, cooling, display refresh rate and upgrade options more important. Use the Gaming Laptop Guide as a checklist.
