SSD vs HDD: Which Storage Is Better?

A straightforward comparison of two common storage technologies and the situations where each can make sense.

SSD

An SSD stores data using solid-state memory and has no moving mechanical parts. SSDs are commonly associated with fast access and quiet operation.

HDD

An HDD stores data on spinning magnetic disks and uses moving mechanical components. HDDs can provide substantial capacity and can be attractive when capacity per cost is the main priority.

Quick comparison

FactorSSDHDD
SpeedGenerally faster accessGenerally slower access
DurabilityNo moving mechanical partsContains moving mechanical parts
CapacityAvailable in a wide range of capacitiesAvailable in a wide range of high capacities
NoiseSilent operationMechanical operation can produce noise
Power usageOften uses less power than a comparable mechanical driveMechanical operation can require more power
PriceUsually costs more per unit of capacityOften costs less per unit of capacity
Suitable use casesFast everyday computing, laptops and responsive application storageLarge-capacity storage and situations where cost per capacity is important

Which should you choose?

For a laptop where responsiveness and quiet operation matter, an SSD is often a practical choice. For large amounts of storage where capacity and cost are the main considerations, an HDD can still be useful. Your workload and budget should decide the balance.
