Monitor Buying Guide

Understand the main monitor specifications and choose based on whether you need a display for productivity, gaming or a mix of both.

Screen size

Screen size affects how much physical space you have for windows and content. Choose a size that fits your desk and viewing distance.

Resolution

Resolution describes the number of pixels displayed. Higher resolutions can show more detail, but they can also increase the graphics workload for gaming.

Refresh rate

Refresh rate describes how often the screen can update. A higher refresh rate can make motion appear smoother when the content and hardware can take advantage of it.

Panel types

Different panel technologies can balance viewing angles, contrast, color characteristics and response behavior differently. Consider the tasks most important to you.

Response time

Response time describes how quickly pixels can change between states. It can be relevant to motion clarity, particularly for gaming.

Connectivity

Check the inputs and outputs you need for your computer and other devices. Matching the monitor's ports with your hardware avoids unnecessary adapters.

Ergonomics

Look at stand adjustment, height, tilt and the available desk space. A comfortable setup matters for long productivity sessions.

Gaming vs productivity

Gaming

Pay particular attention to refresh rate, response characteristics, resolution, connectivity and whether your graphics hardware can drive the display effectively.

Productivity

Screen size, resolution, viewing comfort, ergonomics and connectivity may matter more for documents, coding, browsing and office workflows.
